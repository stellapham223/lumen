## 6. FAQ schema (8 items, Tier 2 hub)

**Q: How long is the ovulatory phase?**
A: The ovulatory phase is short: 24 to 48 hours of actual ovulation, with the estrogen-and-testosterone peak extending the "ovulatory window" to roughly 3 days. On a standard 28-day cycle, this is days 14 to 16. On longer or shorter cycles, ovulation typically occurs about 14 days before the next period starts, not 14 days after the last one began.

**Q: What hormones peak during the ovulatory phase?**
A: Estrogen reaches its cycle high right before ovulation, then drops sharply. Luteinizing hormone (LH) surges 24 to 36 hours before egg release and triggers ovulation itself. Testosterone has a small parallel peak. FSH has a smaller secondary spike. Progesterone remains low until after ovulation, when the corpus luteum begins producing it.

**Q: How do I know I am in the ovulatory phase?**
A: The most reliable signs are cervical mucus turning clear and stretchy ("egg white" texture), a positive LH urine strip (99% accurate for the surge), and a slight basal body temperature rise the day after ovulation. Mittelschmerz (one-sided lower abdominal twinge) is reliable for the 20% of women who notice it. For scheduling work, tracking cycle day from your last period start is sufficient.

**Q: Why do I feel so confident around ovulation?**
A: Estrogen and testosterone both peak in the ovulatory window. Estrogen supports serotonin and dopamine signaling, which lifts mood and verbal fluency. Testosterone supports drive, libido, and risk tolerance. Brain imaging studies (Pritschet et al. 2024) show that whole-brain dynamical complexity is highest pre-ovulation, consistent with a more outwardly engaged, performance-ready cognitive state. The effect is real but small; do not expect a different person, expect a slight tailwind.

**Q: What kind of work is the ovulatory phase good for?**
A: High-stakes, outward-facing, verbally demanding work: presentations, negotiations, networking, sales pitches, podcast interviews, performance reviews you initiate, and difficult conversations you've been deferring. The verbal-fluency and confidence boost are small but consistent in the literature, and the energy peak supports the higher cognitive load these tasks require.

**Q: What should I avoid scheduling during ovulation?**
A: When you have a choice, defer solo deep work, detail-heavy data analysis, and quiet research sessions to the menstrual or luteal phases. The brain in ovulation tends toward outward engagement and resists sustained inward focus. None of this is catastrophic, just suboptimal. If your schedule has no flex, the workaround is environmental: closed door, headphones, single-tab discipline.

**Q: Is it true that ACL injury risk is higher during ovulation?**
A: Yes. Multiple studies since Wojtys et al. 1998 have found a measurable increase in non-contact ACL injury risk around ovulation, attributed to estrogen's effect on ligament laxity. The relative risk is elevated, but absolute risk per workout remains small. Mitigations: extended warm-up, avoid setting personal records on cuts and pivots during this window, prioritize stable lifts over unstable plyometrics.

**Q: Does the ovulatory phase exist on hormonal birth control?**
A: No. Combined oral contraceptives, the hormonal IUD (in most users), the implant, the patch, and the ring all suppress ovulation. There is no estrogen peak, no LH surge, no testosterone bump. The cycle-syncing framing of "schedule your big presentation in your ovulatory week" does not apply on hormonal birth control. Lumen has a dedicated guide for cycle syncing on birth control covering this.
