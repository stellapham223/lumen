# Anovulatory cycle

An **anovulatory cycle** is a menstrual cycle in which [ovulation](/glossary/ovulation) does not occur. The follicular phase may stretch out without a clear [LH surge](/glossary/lh-surge), or a follicle may develop without rupturing. Bleeding can still happen, but it is triggered by an estrogen drop rather than progesterone withdrawal from a [corpus luteum](/glossary/corpus-luteum).

Anovulatory cycles are more common than most people realize. Studies suggest healthy adults under 40 may have 1 to 2 anovulatory cycles per year even with otherwise regular periods.

## Why anovulatory cycles happen

The mechanism varies, but the common thread is disruption of the [HPO axis](/glossary/hpo-axis) feedback loop that drives ovulation. The most frequent causes:

- **Adolescence.** It takes 1 to 2 years post-[menarche](/glossary/menarche) for cycles to become reliably ovulatory.
- **[PCOS](/glossary/pcos).** Often presents with irregular or chronically absent ovulation.
- **[Perimenopause](/glossary/perimenopause).** Anovulatory cycles become more common as [ovarian reserve](/glossary/ovarian-reserve) declines.
- **High stress, sleep loss, or acute illness.** Can suppress the LH surge in any individual cycle.
- **Under-fueling or over-training.** Can cause [hypothalamic amenorrhea](/glossary/hypothalamic-amenorrhea) or [RED-S](/glossary/red-s).
- **Thyroid disorders.** Both hyper and hypothyroidism can disrupt ovulation ([thyroid cycle interactions](/glossary/thyroid-cycle)).
- **[Hyperprolactinemia](/glossary/prolactin).** Elevated prolactin suppresses GnRH.
- **Hormonal birth control.** Most combined methods deliberately suppress ovulation.
- **Postpartum and breastfeeding.** Cycle return often includes anovulatory cycles in the first few months.

## How to identify one

Anovulatory cycles often look normal on a calendar, so identifying them requires direct ovulation evidence:

- **[BBT](/glossary/basal-body-temperature) tracking.** No sustained temperature rise in the second half of the cycle.
- **[LH testing](/glossary/opk).** No clear surge across the expected ovulation window.
- **Mid-luteal progesterone test.** Blood progesterone under 3 ng/mL roughly 7 days after suspected ovulation indicates no ovulation.
- **Cycle pattern clues.** Cycles consistently under 21 days or over 35 days, or wide month-to-month swings.

A single anovulatory cycle is usually not concerning. A pattern of them (three or more in a row, or chronic irregularity) warrants a clinician conversation.

## What still happens hormonally

In an anovulatory cycle, the [follicular phase](/glossary/follicular-phase) hormone profile (rising estrogen) often still occurs. What is missing is the post-ovulation [progesterone](/glossary/progesterone) surge from the corpus luteum. This has practical consequences:

- The [endometrium](/glossary/endometrium) may keep thickening under unopposed estrogen
- No progesterone-driven [BBT](/glossary/basal-body-temperature) rise
- Bleeding (if it occurs) is breakthrough bleeding from an estrogen drop, not true [menstruation](/glossary/menstruation)
- PMS symptoms may be milder or absent (PMS is driven largely by progesterone metabolites)

Chronically unopposed estrogen (over many anovulatory cycles in a row) raises the risk of endometrial hyperplasia, which is why chronically anovulatory conditions like untreated [PCOS](/glossary/pcos) are flagged for monitoring.

## Anovulatory cycles and fertility

If conception is the goal, anovulatory cycles are the primary barrier. Tracking [BBT](/glossary/basal-body-temperature), [LH](/glossary/opk), and [cervical mucus](/glossary/cervical-mucus-tracking) gives the most reliable picture. If multiple cycles in a row show no ovulation evidence, a fertility workup is the next step.

## Anovulatory cycles and cycle syncing

The four-phase [cycle syncing](/glossary/cycle-syncing) model assumes a natural ovulatory cycle. In an anovulatory cycle:

- The hormonal [luteal phase](/glossary/luteal-phase) does not occur
- The post-ovulation pattern (progesterone-driven detail focus, then late-luteal PMS) is absent
- Calendar-based phase prediction becomes unreliable

For users with chronic anovulation (PCOS, perimenopause, recovery from hypothalamic amenorrhea), the [PCOS cycle syncing guide](/glossary/cycle-syncing-pcos) and [perimenopause cycle syncing guide](/glossary/cycle-syncing-perimenopause) cover adapted protocols.
